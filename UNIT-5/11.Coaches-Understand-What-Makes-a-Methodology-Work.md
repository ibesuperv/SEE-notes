# Coaches Understand What Makes a Methodology Work

## 1. Introduction: "Doing" Agile vs. "Being" Agile

Doing Agile means simply following Agile practices such as Daily Stand-ups, Sprint Planning, and User Stories without understanding their purpose.

Being Agile means understanding and applying the Agile principles and values behind those practices, leading to effective collaboration and continuous improvement.

An Agile Coach understands that a methodology works only when the team understands the purpose behind Agile practices. The coach helps the team move from "Doing Agile" to "Being Agile."

To achieve this, an Agile Coach uses the **Triad of Methodology**, which consists of Practices, Principles, and Values.

---

### 2.1 Practices: "What We Do"

Practices are the visible activities and techniques followed by the team.
Examples include Daily Stand-ups, User Stories, Sprint Planning, Pair Programming, and WIP Limits.

- **The Problem:** If a team follows practices blindly without understanding their purpose, these activities become useless rules and add unnecessary extra work.

### 2.2 Principles: "How We Think"

Principles are the guidelines that explain how Agile practices should be applied. They help teams make better decisions and solve problems. Examples include delivering working software frequently, welcoming changing requirements, and maintaining transparency.

- **The Problem:** Principles are just guidelines. If a team lacks core values like trust and respect, they will ignore the principles when making decisions.

### 2.3 Values: "Why We Care"

Values are the core beliefs that form the foundation of Agile. They include trust, collaboration, respect, courage, commitment, and focus. Without these values, Agile principles and practices cannot be applied effectively.

---

## 3. The Agile Coach's Strategy

To make a methodology work, the coach employs the following strategies:

1.  **Diagnose Failures at the Correct Layer:** When a practice fails, the coach does not just force the team to follow the rules harder. Instead, they look down the chain: Is there a lack of value, principle.
2.  **Coach the Values First:** Coach the values first by building trust, collaboration, and a safe working environment.
3.  **Make Policies Explicit:** When introducing a practice, the coach clearly communicates the _principle_ it supports.
4.  **Promote Self-Organization:** Allow the team to adapt their own practices once they truly understand the underlying Agile values and principles.

# The Principles of Coaching

## 1. Introduction: John Wooden’s Influence on Agile Coaching

The Principles of Coaching are guidelines that help an Agile Coach build a successful Agile team. These principles are based on the coaching philosophy of John Wooden and focus on continuous improvement, teamwork, and effective Agile practices.

---

## 2. The 5 Principles of Coaching

The five coaching principles adapted from John Wooden are:

### 2.1 Industriousness (Hard Work)

An Agile Coach encourages the team to work hard and continuously improve. Team members should actively participate in Sprint Planning, Retrospectives, and other Agile activities to improve their skills and performance.

### 2.2 Enthusiasm (Positive Attitude)

The coach should maintain a positive attitude and motivate the team during Agile adoption. Enthusiasm encourages collaboration, increases confidence, and helps the team overcome challenges.

### 2.3 Condition (Mental and Work Environment)

The coach should maintain a healthy work environment by promoting a sustainable pace, reducing stress, and creating psychological safety. This enables team members to work effectively without burnout.

### 2.4 Fundamentals (Mastery of the Basics)

The coach ensures that the team first masters the basic Agile practices such as Daily Stand-ups, User Stories, Sprint Planning, and WIP limits before adopting advanced techniques.

### 2.5 Development of Team Spirit (Team Work)

The coach promotes collaboration, collective ownership, and shared responsibility among team members. The entire team works together to achieve common project goals instead of focusing on individual success.
